# ADR 0001: Identify one Stage target and its output boundary

- Status: Accepted for project foundation
- Date: 2026-11-23
- Basis: User goal and supplied research, sections 1, 10, and 12

## Context

“Rhodes Mark I” spans physical regulation, aging, replacement parts, and multiple
electronic configurations. The supplied research recommends establishing one
73-key Stage specimen before adding named Suitcase families. No specimen has yet
been supplied. A generic timbre cannot establish specimen fidelity.

## Decision

Use one identified 73-key Mark I Stage as the first measurement target. Begin at
loaded harp terminals with a specified load, then extend to that specimen's
passive Stage controls, cable, and receiver. Preserve explicit boundary names.
Keep Peterson and Janus/Haigler circuitry as distinct later profiles. Speaker,
room, and microphone modeling require a later acoustic-scope decision.

## Consequences

Identity and acquisition are recorded blockers where appropriate. Synthetic
fixtures can support algorithm development but cannot complete hardware gates.
The first decisive result is one note predicting held-out strikes, release,
retrigger, geometry, and loads. Full-keyboard and circuit-family claims follow
additional evidence. The target may be revised when an actual specimen is
available; record that revision rather than silently relabeling existing data.

## Alternatives considered

A broad “all Mark I variants” model lacks an identifiable initial acceptance
boundary. Starting with a complete Suitcase acoustic chain introduces additional
unknowns before the note generator and load are identified. Both can become later
product scopes after their measurement and validation requirements are defined.
