## Implemented representation

`KeyHighlight` now additionally exposes:

```cpp
std::array<HighlightPath, 2> visible_paths;
std::size_t visible_path_count;
```

Each `HighlightPath` has a bounded sixteen-point array and a `size`. The existing
`outline`, `cutouts`, `cutout_count`, and `stroke_outline` fields remain available.
The original `photo_geometry.hpp` hit polygons and ownership rules are unchanged.

For white keys, a constexpr boundary arrangement splits original white edges
and neighboring black edges at their proper intersections. Each intersection
has one shared node; connections use node indices, without approximate endpoint
matching. Exposed white segments run forwards, and exposed black segments run
backwards. Traversing the resulting directed cycles produces the actual visible
contours. There are no retained white-outline edges inside black interiors and
no cancellation between outline and cutout paths during rendering.

The full table contains 82 contours for 73 keys. Nine upper-register white keys
have a small detached rear sliver, so a single contour for every white key would
lose valid visible geometry. The current maximum is eleven vertices in a
contour. Storage and construction are bounded, constexpr and allocation-free.
A compile-time assertion rejects an empty or over-capacity generated result.
Every contour has positive winding, and separate contours have disjoint interiors.
Black keys have one contour with their exact original four coordinates.

The builder is deliberately tied to the existing photo geometry: white top
surfaces and black quadrilaterals are convex, black keys remain above the white
front faces, relevant crossings are proper, and neighboring blacks are separate.
The builder checks its surface/front-face condition and graph/storage bounds.
Tests independently enforce coverage against every black key. Future geometry
with endpoint or collinear contacts needs an explicit topology implementation;
the new builder does not perturb geometry with an epsilon.

## Renderer handoff

Construct closed subpaths from
`shape.visible_paths[0..shape.visible_path_count)` and draw that graphics path
with ordinary `CDrawContext::kPathFilled`. Do not also submit the legacy outline
or cutouts. Retain nonintegral drawing coordinates and use `stroke_outline`
only for black keys. The integrator owns this renderer change.

Closed contours necessarily share the mathematical boundary between black and
white regions. Tests resolve those measure-zero ownership ties using the
unchanged black-first rule, while separately requiring that no black-boundary
sample lies inside a white contour. Actual antialias coverage at exposed edges
remains a native-raster question. This work does not claim the native residuals
are already cleared.

## Independent tests and results

The existing five selectors now exercise the visible contours. They retain the
original exact hit-polygon and legacy cutout checks, and add bounded-path,
positive-winding, self-intersection, detached-contour, preserved-area and
black-boundary checks. For each white contour edge and every black polygon, an
independent outer-half-plane check separates the entire segment from the black
interior; edge samples also verify the result. These checks retain the existing
`1e-8` geometric boundary classification tolerance. No tolerance was relaxed,
and no native pixel-difference threshold was changed.

The dense grid compares ordinary visible-contour coverage against `hit_key`
over 201,298 nonboundary points, with one separately classified boundary point.
Every one of the 73 visible key regions receives at least 100 samples. All
16-by-16 interior grids for all thirty black keys remain excluded from every
white highlight. The twelve recorded native residual coordinates now have
neither white-contour interior nor white-contour boundary at their associated
held-white key. An area comparison against the independently constructed legacy
clipped overlaps also retains tiny rear slivers that a coarse grid might miss.
