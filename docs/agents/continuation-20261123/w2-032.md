## Independent fixture reasoning

For two free masses, `mu = m_h*m_r/(m_h+m_r) = 0.0016 kg` and
`omega = sqrt(k/mu)`, with `k = 20000 N/m` and initial relative speed `1 m/s`.
The conservative compression is `sin(omega*t)/omega`. Its maximum is
`0.000282842712474619 m`, force `5.65685424949238 N`, at
`pi/(2*omega) = 0.0004442882938158366 s`. Both maxima are checked at two
requested maximum steps, 37 and 11 microseconds.

For damping `c = 40000 N s/m^2`, let `u = 1 + c*w/k` and `u0 = 3`.
Integrating the relative mechanics gives
`delta^2 = (2*mu*k/c^2)*(u0-u-log(u0/u))`.
The loading force stationary point satisfies
`3*(u-1) = 2*(u0-1)-2*log(u0/u)`.
Scalar bisection gives its velocity and force; Simpson quadrature of
`dt/dw = -mu/(delta*(k+c*w))` gives time, using `x = sqrt(1-w)` to remove
the initial integrable singularity. The 1024- and 2048-panel results agree within
`2e-14 s`. No candidate evaluator, RHS, root finder, or copied time solver is
used as the oracle. Expected force maximum is approximately
`6.19180727860462 N` at `0.000177690562257462 s`; compression maximum is
`0.000189882880885233 m` at `0.000345059737960216 s`.
Tests verify that the force peak is away from accepted quarter-interval samples.

The prescribed-face fixture sets damper modal projection to zero, so the contact
point is stationary and `delta = 0.0003 - 0.0001*cos(2000*t+0.137)` exactly.
Its relative acceleration is the negative face acceleration. Analytic scalar
root finding predicts force `6.04834755893605 N` at
`0.00104588688007747 s`; the opposite acceleration sign gives a derivative
above `1000 N/s` there, making this fixture discriminate that sign error.
Compression maximum is `0.0004 m` at `0.00150229632679490 s`.

Separate hammer and damper velocity-only actions test both increasing after-side
and decreasing before-side maxima, exact action brackets, preserved mechanical
coordinates, and zero-force clipped after-sides. The damper velocity breakpoint
also preserves the complete instantaneous energy ledger.
