# A023 — Shared nonlinear circuit fidelity

**Finding.** The [architecture](../../ARCHITECTURE.md) and [roadmap](../../ROADMAP.md) correctly require shared stages and separately identified circuit families. The [current engine](../../../src/research_engine.cpp) stops at a synthetic resistive loaded-harp boundary; it implements neither shared amplification nor supply dynamics. Preserve that boundary explicitly. Future nonlinear stages must receive the connected network's terminal signal, with its actual source impedance, rather than an arbitrary voice sum. Local pickup nonlinearities remain separate physical devices.

**Synthetic multitone counterexample.** Specify two voltage sources, `v1=A cos(2π·700t)` and `v2=A cos(2π·1100t)`, driving the same memoryless fixture `f(v)=v+βv³`, with `A` in volts and synthetic nonzero `β` in inverse square volts. Algebra gives `f(v1+v2)−f(v1)−f(v2)=3β(v1²v2+v1v2²)`. Shared processing therefore produces 300, 1500, 2500, and 2900 Hz components, each with peak magnitude `3|β|A³/4`; separately distorted voices lack these components. Use coherent sampling, adequate internal bandwidth, and rate convergence. This proposed fixture isolates topology semantics; it establishes no historical distortion law.

**Supply coupling.** Add a sustained small probe plus a strong gated tone. Observe probe gain/phase and recovery alongside rail voltage and aggregate load current. A shared finite-impedance supply can couple branches through stored state even when their signal paths are separate. Independent per-voice rails erase that mechanism.
